Services Offered

Property & Conveyancing is the heart of Nyama Law Chambers' practice. Whether you are buying your first home, expanding a commercial portfolio, restructuring family land or registering a bond — every step is handled with rigour and clarity.

  • Residential conveyancing: purchases, sales, transfers between family members
  • Business & commercial conveyancing: office buildings, retail, industrial property
  • Mortgage bonds & bond cancellations in coordination with lenders
  • Transfers of equity following divorce, inheritance, or co-ownership changes
  • Commercial & residential leases for landlords and tenants
  • Subdivisions, consolidations & boundary disputes
  • Title deed rectifications and lost-deed reconstructions
  • Property estate planning integrated with wills and trusts

Our Conveyancing Process

Every transfer follows a clear, transparent six-step process. You will know what is happening at each stage.

01
Offer & Instruction

You instruct us. We review the offer to purchase or sale agreement and explain your obligations clearly before signature.

02
Due Diligence

We confirm clear title, check for outstanding rates and bond cancellations, and obtain rates clearance from the local authority.

03
Bond Coordination

If finance is involved, we coordinate with your bank's bond attorney so transfer and bond can lodge together.

04
Signing & Payment

Documents are signed; purchase price is held in our trust account; stamp duty is calculated and paid to ZIMRA.

05
Deeds Lodgement

The signed documents are lodged at the Deeds Registry. We track the file daily.

06
Registration & Handover

Once registered, you receive your title deed. Keys, statements and full file delivered to you.

A note on conveyancing trust funds: All payments related to your conveyancing transaction pass through our regulated trust account — never directly to or from the other party. This is a protection prescribed by the Law Society of Zimbabwe and is non-negotiable.

Approach: Negotiated resolution where possible
Litigation: Vigorous representation when required

A Different Philosophy

The breakdown of a relationship is often one of the most difficult periods in a person's life — emotionally, financially, and legally. We bring a different philosophy to it. We are litigators, fully capable of fierce courtroom representation, but our default is collaborative.

What is Collaborative Family Law?

A dignified, non-adversarial route to resolving family matters

Collaborative law is a process in which specially-trained family lawyers help couples and families manage separation in a dignified way — without the intervention or threat of court proceedings unless absolutely necessary. The aim is settlement that works for you, your family, and your children.

Areas We Cover

  • Separation — formalising arrangements where divorce is not yet underway
  • Divorce — civil marriages, customary unions, and the financial aspects
  • Arrangements for Children — custody, guardianship, access, maintenance
  • Cohabitation & Pre-nuptial Agreements
  • Domestic Violence Protection Orders
  • Matrimonial Property Disputes
  • Change of Name applications
  • Family Mediation & Arbitration

When We Recommend Litigation

Collaboration is our default — but it requires both parties to come to the table in good faith. We will recommend formal litigation where there is domestic violence, financial concealment, refusal to negotiate, or urgent need for court protection.

Confidentiality: All consultations with our Family Department are protected by legal privilege. Initial conversations regarding separation, abuse, or family matters are held in strict confidence.

What We Offer

A valid will is the single most important legal document many Zimbabweans never create. Our Wills & Estate Department helps clients prepare for the future and protects their families from the painful disputes that arise when no instructions exist.

  • Drafting and execution of wills compliant with the Wills Act [Chapter 6:06]
  • Living trusts, family trusts and charitable trusts
  • General and special powers of attorney
  • Full estate administration as executor
  • Resealing of foreign wills in Zimbabwe
  • Estate disputes, capacity challenges, will contests
  • Integrated estate & property planning
